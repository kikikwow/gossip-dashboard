Shein was worth $100 billion in 2022. It went public at $26.5 billion, and still could not get a first-day pop. Four years, three countries, and a 73% markdown later, the world's biggest fast fashion company finally listed in Hong Kong on September 1. The founders: - Sky Xu, also known as Chris Xu, started a cross-border e-commerce business in Nanjing around 2008 - He launched Shein in 2012 with three partners: Maggie Gu, Molly Miao and Tony Ren - The model: $5 dresses and $10 jeans, shipped from Chinese suppliers to about 160 countries - Xu almost never gives interviews. Even his own prospectus reveals little about him - The company moved its headquarters to Singapore in late 2021 The long road to a listing: - November 2023: filed for a US IPO. Lawmakers and regulators pushed back over labour and transparency - 2024 and 2025: tried London instead. MPs questioned the exchange's vetting of the company - July 2025: filed confidentially in Hong Kong - July 10, 2026: China's securities regulator approved the plan - September 1, 2026: shares started trading under ticker 0625 The valuation ladder: - April 2022: about $100B, the peak, when it was worth more than H&M and Zara's parents - May 2023: $66B, with Mubadala, General Atlantic and Sequoia China - August 2026: priced at HK$48.56 a share, roughly $26.5B - The 2023 investors are down about 60%. The 2022 backers, about 73% Why the price fell so far: - 2025 revenue grew just 8%, to $41.8B - Net income fell 39%, from $3.4B to $2.1B - In Q1 2026, revenue grew 1.1% and the company swung to a $99M loss - The US closed the de minimis exemption that let small parcels arrive duty-free - Europe, its biggest market, opened a formal investigation in February - Temu is fighting it for the same customers The deal: - Raised about $1.7B by selling roughly 6.6% of the company - Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan were joint sponsors - Existing backers Boyu Capital, Tiger Global and General Atlantic anchored $383M as cornerstones - Public shares carry one-tenth the votes of founder shares - The four co-founders keep 90% of voting control, and are locked up until September 2028 Then came the debut: - Opened around HK$44.62, 8% below the IPO price - Fell as far as HK$43.72, down 10% - Clawed back to close at HK$48.50, almost exactly flat - Saxo's Charu Chanana: "investors still don't see Shein as obviously cheap" - Meanwhile, Hong Kong AI and robotics deals were hundreds of times oversubscribed Sky Xu's wealth: - He owns about 30% of the company - That was worth more than $23B at the 2022 peak, per Bloomberg - At the IPO price, roughly $8B The frame: Shein grew revenue every year and stayed profitable through 2025. But it missed the window. The listing it planned at $100B in New York happened at a quarter of that in Hong Kong. Investors were happy to pay up for AI. A $40B-revenue retailer with shrinking margins was priced like one.
